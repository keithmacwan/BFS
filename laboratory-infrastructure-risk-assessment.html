Protect the systems your science depends on

The risk is often in the gap between systems.

Laboratories rely on tightly connected building systems to maintain temperature, humidity, ventilation, pressure relationships, power quality, cold-storage conditions and alarm visibility. A weakness in any one of these systems can place high-value samples, specialized equipment, research schedules and operational continuity at risk.

  • A freezer may have emergency power while its upstream distribution still contains an unrecognized single point of failure.
  • A BAS may monitor a room, while the alarm delay, priority or escalation path does not reflect the scientific risk.
  • An HVAC system may meet normal operating needs, while failure response depends on undocumented manual actions.
  • A critical alarm may reach a control room, while response procedures, contacts or recovery expectations remain unclear.

BFS helps organizations find these vulnerabilities before they become operational events.

BAS & critical alarms

Points, limits, priorities, delays, notification, escalation, acknowledgement and loss-of-monitoring scenarios.

Critical utilities

Chilled water, heating water, steam, purified water, drainage, gases, isolation and shutdown planning.

03

Detection, response & recovery

Determine whether people can act before impact becomes irreversible.

  • Who receives the first notification?
  • Is the message clear enough to support action?
  • What happens if the first recipient does not respond?
  • Are after-hours responsibilities defined?
  • Can responders find the right room, asset, valve, panel or control point?
  • Are temporary measures and escalation paths documented and tested?

The Assessment Process

Discover. Assess. Prioritize. Act.

01

Discover

Define the facility, laboratory functions, critical assets, concerns and business objectives. Gather drawings, BAS/alarm information, equipment inventories, incident history, maintenance records and existing plans.

02

Assess

Review available documentation, interview stakeholders and conduct a targeted site walkdown. Trace critical systems from source to end use and identify failure modes, dependencies and response gaps.

03

Prioritize

Evaluate findings using scientific consequence, operational impact, likelihood, detection capability, redundancy and recovery readiness.

04

Act

Provide a practical roadmap separating immediate operational controls from engineering studies, maintenance actions and longer-term capital projects.

Common Warning Signs

Your facility may benefit from an assessment if…

Critical freezers or laboratory equipment have unclear emergency-power coverage.
Alarm notifications are inconsistent, delayed or dependent on a single person.
Staff receive frequent nuisance alarms and may miss a meaningful event.
Critical room conditions are monitored, but response instructions are unclear.
Drawings, equipment inventories or sequences do not reflect current conditions.
Backup storage exists, but transfer capacity or relocation procedures are unconfirmed.
Expansion or new equipment is planned without a coordinated infrastructure-capacity review.
The organization has experienced a temperature, humidity, pressure, power or water-related event.
Capital needs are known informally but have not been translated into a prioritized risk case.
Facility and laboratory teams know different pieces, but no one has reviewed the complete chain.
